Privacy policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

Locked Site Blocker is a browser extension that blocks websites you choose. In short: everything it knows stays in your browser, and nothing is collected or shared. This page covers the extension and this website.

What the extension stores

  • The list of websites you block and, for each one, when you first added it, when its current timer started and when it ends.
  • For each site on that list, how many times it was opened and stopped by the block, per day for the last 60 days plus a running total. Nothing about the pages themselves is kept, only the counts, and they're deleted when you remove the site.
  • Whether you've dismissed the Incognito notice in the extension's popup, when you first opened the popup, and whether you've answered or dismissed its one-time "Is the blocker helping you?" prompt.
  • When the extension was last running (updated about once a minute, and when you use it) and, for the last 20 times it was switched off, when it went off, when it came back on and whether you've dismissed the notice about it.

This is kept locally in your browser using its extension storage. It isn't synced to your Google or Microsoft account or to other devices.

What the extension doesn't do

  • It doesn't collect, transmit, sell or share any data. It has no servers and makes no network requests of its own.
  • It doesn't read, record or change the content of the pages you visit, and it doesn't keep a history of your browsing (beyond the daily counts above, for sites you've blocked). The only time it looks at a page's address is to block the site you're on: when you open its popup (to offer a one-click "Block" button for that site, which its keyboard shortcut also opens), or when you use its right-click menu. The address isn't kept unless you block the site, and then only the site's name goes on your list.
  • It has no account, ads, analytics or tracking, and it doesn't run remote code.
  • The popup may show a one-time prompt with buttons to rate the extension or support its development. They only open the store page or the donation page in a new tab when you click them; nothing about you is sent with them.

Permissions it uses

  • Blocking websites (declarativeNetRequest): tells the browser which sites on your list to block.
  • Storage (storage): keeps your block list on your device.
  • Right-click menu (contextMenus): adds "Block this site" to the menu you get when you right-click a page or the extension's icon.
  • Alarms (alarms): wakes the extension about once a minute to note that it's running, so it can tell you later if it was switched off, and for how long.
  • Removing blocked sites' service workers (browsingData): when you block a site, removes that site's service worker, a background script some sites install for push alerts or offline reading. Without this, such a site could keep opening from its own cache despite the block. Nothing else is removed: not your history, cookies, sign-ins or the site's other data. That site's push alerts stop until you remove the block and visit it again.
  • Access to all websites (<all_urls>): lets the browser show the extension's countdown page in place of a site on your list, reload tabs already open on a site when you block it, and see which site the current tab is on when you ask to block it. It covers every site because you decide which sites to block.

Deleting your data

Removing a site from the list (possible once its timer has ended) deletes it. Uninstalling the extension deletes all of its data from your browser.

This website

  • The site uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking, and loads no fonts or scripts from other services.
  • It is hosted on GitHub Pages, run by GitHub, Inc. (USA). When you visit, GitHub logs your IP address for security purposes, as it does for every GitHub Pages site. See GitHub's privacy statement.
